They Weren't Asking Me to Execute Anymore — They Were Asking Me to Lead

The moment I realized leadership isn't about doing more — it's about creating the frame within which others can do well.

I kept getting tasks constantly — from management, from the team, from everywhere. I did the technical work: reports, escalations, admin. Everything. The frustration kept building, mine and the team's — I felt like I was pulling in every direction and it still wasn't enough.

One day, a colleague told me directly: "Roxana, we handle the technical work. Your job is to make us visible. Advocate for our work in front of management." The words stopped me cold — I knew he was right. I'd assumed I had to keep doing everything I'd always done, to not lose control, to leave nothing uncovered. But he'd just shown me something essential: they weren't looking to me for execution anymore. They were looking to me for direction and representation.

Leadership isn't about doing more. It's about creating the frame within which others can do well. As a mother, I made the same mistake — I thought my role was to do everything: education, organization, emotions, control. But my role is to build the environment my children grow in, not to be the entire system myself.

In both roles, the lesson was the same: stability doesn't come from carrying everything alone. It comes from building the structure that holds everyone up.

If you're in a role where you've taken on too much and you feel it's time to rethink the system, reach out.
