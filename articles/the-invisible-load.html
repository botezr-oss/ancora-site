The Invisible Load

The emotional load of managing roles, expectations, and the pressure to do it all alone.

A colleague once told me she wanted a baby because, watching me, it looked so easy to work with a small baby around. My son was four months old. I nursed him during meetings. I carried him in a wrap while writing emails. Most of the time he was in my arms, even during video calls. From the outside, it looked simple.

What didn't show was the emotional load I was carrying alone. Years later, those experiences are part of why I became a coach — not because I have all the answers, but because too many women walk a road that many of us walk, yet rarely talk about.

It's not the work itself that wears us down the most. It's the pressure of being, all at once: a good leader, a present mother, a balanced partner — and the feeling that we're supposed to manage it all by ourselves.

If you've ever felt that invisible emotional load — the moment when, from the outside, you seem to have it all together, but inside you're carrying everything alone — I'm curious: when did you feel it most strongly? On your first day back at work? At a promotion? When someone told you, "you're handling this so well"?
