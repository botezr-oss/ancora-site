Not Giving Up on Yourself — Between Life and Leadership

Not giving up on yourself means continuing to look inward with gentleness, keeping the child's curiosity and an open heart.

What does it mean to not give up on yourself, in a world that asks you to follow its rules? For me, it's meant looking inward more often, with the same curiosity a four-year-old has when discovering something new — an innocent curiosity, free of judgment, that simply asks: "What's here?"

It's not about criticizing yourself. It's not about asking "what should I criticize about myself today." It's about looking at every wound and obstacle as steps on a road that shaped who you are today.

On this road, I was the main character in my own story, but also a supporting character in other people's stories. Sometimes, out of a wish to help, I tried to be the hero in someone else's play. I understood, eventually, that I can't live anyone's life for them — everyone gets the lessons and challenges they need for their own mission.

For years, I tried to define myself through society's expectations and the standards of my environment, until the day I realized: the only person who can define my life is me. Lately, I've spent more time with myself, and I discovered that not everything I believed about myself was actually true. Many of those "truths" were, in fact, reactions born from the need to survive certain moments.

As a leader, I've led several teams, each with its own challenges. At first, I believed I wouldn't be able to handle it — imposter syndrome kept whispering that I wasn't enough. And yet, over time, people showed up who believed in me and saw past my mask. Through their eyes, I started to see myself too.

I learned that every person is unique, and that uniqueness can only bloom in the right context. I tested different leadership styles and understood that not everyone reacts the same way. In those moments, the leader carries the weight, holds the direction, protects.

It's hard to be "for everyone" when you're only one person. But it's deeply fulfilling when you discover you can create contexts where both you and others can bloom. Today, for me, not giving up on myself means continuing to look inward with gentleness — keeping the child's curiosity, the leader's experience, and the open heart of someone who knows that real power doesn't come from playing a role for others, but from authentically living the role of your own life.
