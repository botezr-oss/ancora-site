Working Under the New Instability

When corporations sell uncertainty instead of stability, exhaustion comes from fear, not work.

I've talked with a lot of women who work. What's changing isn't just the workload — it's something deeper. Corporations used to sell stability. Today they sell uncertainty: COVID, war, financial crisis, restructuring every six months. Too many years where "it'll get better" turned into "we don't know what's next." So even if you have a paycheck today, the fear of losing it tomorrow becomes constant.

You stop working for growth. You work to survive. You answer emails at 11pm — not because you want to, but because you want to be seen as indispensable. You don't ask for the flexibility you actually need, because you don't want to look less committed. You hide it when your child is sick. You work with a fever. You don't take time off. All of it, to prove: "I'm not on the layoff list."

And slowly, the exhaustion stops being about the work itself. It comes from the constant fear that you're not doing enough to stay. Uncertainty has become the new stability — and it wears you down more than any deadline ever could.

If you have a job today but you're living with the fear of losing it tomorrow, and that fear is exhausting you more than the work itself — reach out. I work with women in leadership who are ready to stop living in constant survival mode.
