The Body Keeps Score

How pushing through 'just a little longer' eventually leads to illness, not just exhaustion.

How many times have you been on the edge of collapsing — physically and emotionally? And how many times did the sudden realization hit: I'm not allowed to. Because without me, everything falls apart. In the middle of a kids' birthday party. While cooking. While driving them to and from activities.

The thought that you can't stop is what makes you push through "just a little longer." That thought is survival mode — an automatic brain mechanism that keeps you running. And it's okay, as long as it doesn't stretch on for years — as long as there's at least one person you allow yourself to ask for help.

Because any prolonged version of "I can push a little more" eventually leads to illness. Not exhaustion. Illness. Your body doesn't distinguish between temporary survival and chronic burnout. It simply keeps score — and one day, it presents the bill.

If "just a little more" has become your only strategy, and you feel your body is already sending you signals you're ignoring — reach out. I work with mothers who are tired of pushing through "just a little more," who want to be present without consuming themselves. The first two 30-minute clarity conversations are free.
