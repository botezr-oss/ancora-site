The Standard That Never Ends

How the expectation to do it all becomes a standard without an endpoint.

As a woman, performance is an expectation. As a mother and a leader, it becomes a standard — not because anyone imposes it on you directly, but because you absorb it without realizing.

Be present at home. Be clear and firm at work. Look good. Don't be "too much," but not "too little" either. And above all, don't make mistakes. So you end up juggling everything — roles, responsibilities, expectations — and even when you're holding it all in the air, the thought still shows up: "It's not enough." Not because you're not doing enough. Because the standard has no end point.

Most people don't need another strategy, or another plate to spin. They need something harder: permission to stop holding all of them. In my sessions, we don't work on "how to do more." We work on seeing differently what you're already doing — because that's where the feeling of "enough" actually starts.

If this sounds familiar, I'm curious: which is the plate that's hardest for you to put down? If you'd like to explore it together, I offer two free 30-minute clarity conversations.
