The Car Is Where I Cried

When the only moment of emotional release is alone in your car, it's time to find a different way to live.

You cried in the car after dropping him off at daycare. You're in a meeting and suddenly remember: you forgot to give him his antibiotic this morning. You put the laundry in and forget it there — again. "I'm a bad mother," you tell yourself. "I can't take care of him the way I want to." And yet you keep going, because "when I think I can't anymore, I can push a little more." Because "it'll be fine, I just need patience until it's my turn."

But the truth is, "your turn" doesn't come on its own. It comes when you decide that exhaustion isn't proof of devotion — it's a signal that you need support.

I work with mothers in leadership who are tired of pushing through "a little more," who want to be present in their lives, whatever role they hold, without consuming themselves. If today you're at the point where "I can push a little more" has become your only strategy — reach out. The first two 30-minute clarity conversations are free, no pressure — just a space where you can breathe.
