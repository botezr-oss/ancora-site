Every Team I Grew, Grew Me Too

How growing a team became a mirror for understanding myself as a mother, a leader, and a person.

I've learned that without people, you don't have a business — and without a business, people have nowhere to work. When I was growing my team, I was growing my family too. The way I treated the people on my team was the way I treated my own children: with respect, with gentleness, with a wish to connect and understand how we could work together. Slowly, everyone found their place — them and me. We were part of a system that only worked if we worked together.

Then came the day I was told: "We don't have projects anymore. Your role isn't needed." The company closed. I stayed home to enjoy my family — the first three months were about the joy of those moments with them. After that came the harder fight: with myself, with what I wanted, with what I actually knew how to do. Job descriptions had become vague. I didn't know if I fit anywhere. Everything felt like a roll of the dice.

So I decided I would create an environment of safety — except that goal was just as vague, until I understood something: what I know how to do best is be a mother, and grow teams that perform. A mother to my children. A leader to the people who were on my team. And with that came the realization of how much weight a mother in a manager or leader role actually carries.

That's when I decided mothers need support too — or rather, that no mother should have to carry it alone. Not because she can't manage or can't do it, but because, every so often, she needs space to just be herself, fully, and remember who she is.

If you're a mother in leadership or at home, employed or in transition, and you feel you need space to be you — reach out.
