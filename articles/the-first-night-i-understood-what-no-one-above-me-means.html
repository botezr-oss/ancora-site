The First Night I Understood What 'No One Above Me' Means

The moment you realize there's no one 'above' anymore — and the weight that comes with it.

The first night home with my newborn — he cried in the middle of the night. I woke up and waited: someone had to know what to do. And then I realized: that someone was me.

Years later, the same feeling, when I became a team leader. I wasn't the colleague anymore. I was the responsibility. I was the final decision.

There's a moment in life when you understand that there's no one "above" anymore — that you are the structure, you are the stability, you are the reference point. And with that comes the pressure: don't make a mistake, don't hesitate, don't show doubt.

But real maturity isn't perfection. It's the ability to stay present inside the responsibility. Leadership and motherhood don't call for superheroes — they call for people who can carry their own strength without fracturing on the inside.

If you're in a role where many people depend on you, and you want to grow your capacity to carry that responsibility without erasing yourself — reach out. I work with women in leadership and motherhood who want to carry the weight without losing themselves in it.
