Surprise: The Emotion That Breaks Our Rhythm and Invites a Choice

Surprise carries the potential for choice — when we can consciously respond instead of react.

I've always experienced surprise as an emotion that freezes the mind for a moment — a sudden, brief pause, where something unexpected enters our reality and shifts our state.

Sometimes joy follows surprise right away — good news, an unplanned meeting, a tender gesture. Other times, sadness, anger, or frustration follow — because what arrived didn't match what we had in mind.

Surprise is short-lived, but its impact is instant. If I've made a plan, set a clear direction, and suddenly something unforeseen shows up — "a surprise" — my mind can interpret that event as a danger. Not because it is one, but because it's different from what I anticipated. The emotional brain reacts not to reality, but to the loss of control over prediction.

As a parent, I've learned that surprise moments are a constant — something falls, someone spills, something happens differently than planned. In those moments, we have two options: consciously choose how to live the next moment, or react automatically, using lines and gestures we once saw as children — even if we promised ourselves we wouldn't repeat them.

Surprise carries the potential for a choice within it. But for that choice to be a conscious one, we need to recognize the instant our mind wants to run, control, or react. We need to ask ourselves, even for a second: is this really a danger, or just a moment of discomfort?

Getting to a rational place when a surprise is unwelcome isn't simple — it takes years of inner work, introspection, gentleness, and practice. But it's possible. And with every conscious choice, we don't just handle surprises better — we become more present in our own lives.
