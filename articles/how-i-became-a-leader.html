How I Became a Leader

A 12+ year journey of learning about myself, others, and how we grow together.

I like the word "leader." For me, it's not just a title — it's part of who I am. I choose it because I have a deep passion for working with people, discovering their potential, and identifying the environments where they can do their best work.

The road here wasn't short. It took more than 12 years of working on myself to be able to work authentically with others. I read widely — psychology, personal development, spirituality, philosophy. I went through leadership and coaching training. I sought out people who could show me new perspectives, and learned from every challenge. I tested things, got them wrong, tried something else, and kept searching, again and again.

My first year as a team leader, and later my first year as a business manager, were probably the hardest. I wanted to quit hundreds of times. And yet, every time, the right people would show up — telling me what I had to offer was needed, that I could help others.

Every change of role, team, or company brought situations I wasn't ready for. I learned that people development and business development have to stay in balance — you can't run projects without people, and you can't work with people without projects. There were moments I had to split myself between the team and business goals, between work and home, where my children needed their own space of safety and balance. I gave everything I had, until the moment I couldn't anymore.

That's when I started asking myself the essential questions: Where's my limit? Where am I, in everything I do? Who am I, in everything I do? It took months to feel like myself again — to accept that I can't be everywhere, that not everything is my responsibility, and that it's healthy to share the load and look for people who can help balance me.

Today, I choose consciously how I invest my energy. I give pro bono hours to those who need it, through Bridging Gaps, and I put my experience as a leader and coach at the disposal of those who want to discover who they are and where they're headed. For me, being a leader means always being in the process of learning — about yourself, about others, and about how we can grow together.
