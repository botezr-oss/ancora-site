Why I Chose the Path of Coaching

Structure creates clarity — and clarity is essential when someone is moving through discomfort.

Working with people doesn't just mean being physically present. It means being truly present — listening to what's said, and to what isn't. Seeing people fully, and walking alongside them on their journey of discovery, so they can find their own answers.

I always wanted to build an environment of balance around me. But to offer that, I first had to find balance within myself. I read widely on personal development, psychology, and constellations. I tested, learned through experience, and worked with people eager to grow. And still, I felt something was missing.

What I was missing was structure. So I chose to pursue coaching training. I wanted to learn how to structure my thinking and train my attention, to be truly present in the conversations that mattered. That's when I discovered that structure creates clarity — and clarity is essential, especially when someone is moving through discomfort and looking for a way out.

Coaching isn't psychology, and it doesn't set out to resolve trauma or the past. It's about partnership. About walking the talk. About being alongside someone in a safe space, where they can explore options, discover new perspectives, and choose the path that fits them. Through my training, I learned a method that convinced me real transformation starts with three fundamental steps: Awareness — understanding what you truly feel, think, and want. Clarity — bringing order to your thoughts and seeing your options clearly. Choice — owning the direction that supports you, and moving forward with confidence.

For me, this was perhaps the most important development decision I've made. In the end, every choice comes down to how we want to live our lives: on autopilot, or consciously.
