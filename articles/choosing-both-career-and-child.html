Choosing Both — Career and Child

How accepting a role without choosing between career and child meant paying an emotional price — and refusing to let guilt be the cost.

I was offered the administrator role. My son was one year old. "It's a huge opportunity," they told me. "You'll have our full support." I was working from home. I wanted to be there for his first steps, his first words — the days you can't get back. If I refused, I'd have to look for another company, go through more interviews, risk losing the ability to work remotely at all.

That's when I understood: I wasn't choosing between career and child. I was choosing between this role, or losing it along with the chance to watch my child grow up. I took the role.

The first twelve months were about rebuilding — creating a safe environment, mutual respect. He was part of the team; he sat in on meetings. Then came the market crisis. Projects became scarce. The company was absorbed into a larger group. Restructuring. Roles cut. Relationships on the team broken.

And suddenly, the role I'd taken to keep my child close became the role where I had to make decisions that affected entire families. I paid an emotional price for every day I carried both the weight of the business and the care of my child at the same time. But the truth is, I never chose between them. I refused to accept that I had to.

If you're at a point where you feel you have to choose between career and child — and you know you don't want to — reach out. I work with mothers in leadership who want both, without guilt and without burning out.
