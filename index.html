About me

Years of watching teams — how they form, where they get stuck, what holds them together — have turned into intuition. I can sense the dynamics of a team or a situation before they show up in numbers or in open conflict.

My whole career has been about making things work — even when that meant stepping outside corporate rules. Every time, I saw how a rigid system can stop a good person from growing. So in every company, I ended up doing intrapreneurship: creating space where none existed.

The results were visible — teams grew, the business grew, profit grew. But what made me want to keep going wasn't the numbers. It was that the people on my teams understood why things were happening the way they were — they had explanations, transparency, and a sense of control over their own situation.

Now I want to bring that to people I don't lead directly too: not just results for the business, but clarity and transparency for themselves.

Who it's for

I work with people who lead — managers, startup founders, team leads — regardless of company size or job title.

They usually come to me at moments when something feels off, but they can't quite put their finger on what. They have results, they have experience, but they're missing a mirror — someone who can offer clarity before the next big decision.

How we work together

Clarity session

A first meeting, with no pressure and no long-term commitment. Its purpose isn't to solve everything at once — it's to create a space where you can speak freely, in confidence, about what's really on your mind.

For many people, this session is enough to see their problem more clearly. For others, it's the natural first step toward the Recalibration program.

Recalibration program

A process of at least 3 sessions — and it can continue for as long as needed — for people who feel something isn't working, but can't quite name what.

The first two sessions are weekly. The third comes two weeks later — enough time for things to settle and for real clarity to emerge, not just momentary enthusiasm.

By the end, the problem you came in with is no longer a dead end — it's a place from which you can see multiple possible solutions, and the resources you need for them.
